Overview

This is the institutional knowledge base for the DOE GEMS Prize (DrivenData competition 306): predicting geologic faults absent from the USGS/INGENIOUS training catalogue in the GeoDAWN region of Nevada and California.

It is a research-and-documentation agent — it does not generate, validate, or submit prediction files. Submission generation is a separate, explicitly gated process.

Mission: continuously research, organize, and document everything relevant to identifying faults absent from the existing USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue in the GeoDAWN area — and turn that into a rich, navigable, source-linked section of this site. A knowledge-and-hypothesis engine that makes the next modeling decision smarter, not a submission generator.

A. Competition ground truth, data, and official baseline

A1★★★ GEMS Prize problem statement, metric, and submission format
Defines: GeoDAWN region; provided features = surface conductivity + depth to conductive base, detrended elevation + slope, dilatation rate / shear strain rate / second invariant of strain rate, isostatic gravity anomaly + slope, RTP magnetic anomaly, TMI, vertical & horizontal slope of TMI, top-of-crustal magnetic source depth, earthquake density; labels = USGS Quaternary faults + INGENIOUS; external data allowed if licensed; metric DTI α=0.2/β=0.8, R=300 m; submission float32 GeoTIFF EPSG:32611 100 m.

B. Potential-field edge / lineament detection (gravity & magnetics)

Relevant because the provided feature stack ships RTP anomaly, TMI, and the vertical/horizontal slope of TMI — exactly the inputs these methods consume. Tilt-type filters add depth-balanced edges, useful for the "buried fault under basin fill" regime of Basin-and-Range.

B1★★ Tilt-angle family on magnetics — TDR and TAHG, Zanjan case study
Tilt angle = arctan(vertical-derivative / total-horizontal-derivative): depth-insensitive, edges at zero crossings; TAHG (tilt of the total horizontal gradient) equalises shallow/deep sources and puts maxima on edges; used to map basement faults hidden under thick sedimentary cover — the "buried fault" regime of Basin-and-Range basins.
https://jesphys.ut.ac.ir/article_58910.html?lang=en — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: directly applicable to the provided RTP/TMI bands; the depth-insensitivity of tilt is valuable where multiple fault generations overlap.
high — primary journal, read
B2★★ Gravity lineaments via second horizontal derivative, Central Anatolia
Edge operator from the second horizontal derivative of the truncated-plate model; non-maximum suppression + double threshold; explicitly "useful for detecting deep faults even in the presence of shallower features."
https://link.springer.com/article/10.5047/eps.2011.04.003 — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: the provided isostatic gravity anomaly + slope bands are the right input; the deep-fault-in-presence-of-shallow claim matches the GeoDAWN setting.
high — primary, read
B3★ Method comparison — SVD vs tilt angle on gravity
Second vertical derivative enhances shallow edges; tilt balances depths; both place edges at the same cardinal points in their test area. Useful for choosing which derivative layers to stack.
https://www.irejournals.com/formatedpaper/1703006.pdf — read 2026-09-26.
high — primary, read
B4★★ Fault identification suite, Anza Basin (Kenya) — incl. iTilt
Compares 2nd-VDR, THDR, analytic signal, NVDR-THDR, Tilt-Euler and iTilt-Euler; iTilt (Liu et al. 2015) divides VDR by the analytic-signal amplitude to stabilise derivatives over stacked sources — the practical choice where multiple fault generations overlap.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44195-025-00085-x — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: the iTilt stabilisation is the practical choice for the GeoDAWN area, where multiple fault generations are expected to overlap.
high — primary, read
B5★ Integrated satellite-lineament + gravity edges, Gongola Basin
Concordance of directional-filtered imagery lineaments (NW-SE, NE-SW) with second-horizontal-derivative gravity edges; template for the multi-evidence intersection approach.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352938519303684 — read 2026-09-26, abstract level.
medium — abstract-level read

C. DEM / LiDAR scarp detection

The 1 m DEM tiles over the footprint (716 confirmed in the GEMSDOE inventory against the USGS 3DEP bucket) are the richest source for young, unmapped surface faults. Curvature template matching is the most directly usable published method found.

C1★★★ Regional scarp extraction by curvature template matching
Sare, B., et al. 2019, JGR: Solid Earth, doi:10.1029/2018JB016886. Convolve directional DEM curvature with the curvature template of the diffusive scarp-degradation model (Hanks 2000 / Hilley et al. 2010), yielding per-pixel scarp amplitude and misfit on ≤2 m DEMs over ≥100 km regions; validated against field-dated scarps. Its in-paper context reports that up to ~85% of swath pixels are unmapped in the Q-faults DB around real scarps — the same completeness gap this prize scores.
https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JB016886 — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: the single most directly usable DEM method found. The ~85% catalogue incompleteness around real scarps is direct evidence that the catalogue is structurally incomplete — the exact target of this prize.
high — primary, AGU, read
C2★★ Utah Geological Survey — LiDAR as the fault-mapping tool
0.5 m bare-earth DEMs + slopeshade/hillshade reveal "many previously unmapped fault traces", incl. under vegetation and for non-N-S trends; slope-shade manipulation is the practical trick for small scarps.
https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/lidar-tool-for-geologists/ — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: validates DEM-first scanning for the GeoDAWN footprint, including non-N-S trends (relevant given the flight-line striping concern on N-S candidates).
high — official state survey, read
C3★★ Scarps beneath dense canopy — Seattle fault zone
1.5–10 m scarps invisible in the field/air photos, found in leaf-off LiDAR; ground-return density is the resolving constraint. Justifies DEM-first scanning in vegetated parts of the GeoDAWN footprint.
https://pugetsoundlidar.ess.washington.edu/harding.pdf — read 2026-09-26.
high — primary, read
C4★★ Active-trace identification from LiDAR DEM, Yangsan fault (Korea)
Scarp + deflected-stream signatures on 20 cm DEMs; distinguishes fault valleys from fluvial valleys by linear continuity and connectivity — the false-positive discipline a scarp miner needs.
https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/19/4838 — read 2026-09-26.
Relevance: the continuity/connectivity criterion is the false-positive discipline needed when mining DEM scarp candidates at scale.
high — primary, read
C5★ ML scarp detection — review of U-Net-style approaches
Reviews automated scarp detection incl. a U-Net-like network for scarps (cites Su et al. 2023) and VHSR-image rupture detection; positions DEM morphometry + ML as the emerging standard. (Cited-paper details not fetched; the review itself is the source of record here.)
https://www.ijres.org/papers/Volume-13/Issue-5/13052644.pdf — read 2026-09-26.
medium — review read; cited papers not individually fetched

D1★★★ Play-fairway analysis, Brady's & Astor Pass (Great Basin)
Siler, D.L., and J.E. Faulds, 2013. Permeability-potential model from fault zone, minor faults, and micro-earthquake activity; heat model from gradients, springs, alteration, He isotopes, magnetics. This is the DOE-blessed factor stack for "where do fluids flow along faults" — the geothermal relevance filter the prize cares about.
OSTI record; Science.gov landing: https://www.science.gov/topicpages/g/geothermal+system+exploration — record retrieved 2026-09-26.

Faulds, J.E., et al. 2016, Stanford Geothermal Workshop. Note the overlap with the competition's provided bands (strain rates, earthquake density, gravity, conductivity): the feature stack was chosen to reproduce exactly this methodology.

E. Catalogue-gap reasoning — highest-leverage domain

The actual scored target is not "what looks fault-like" — it is what the catalogue is structurally likely to have missed. This section records the methodology of the INGENIOUS compilation and the USGS QFFDB themselves (not just their output rasters), and uses that to reason about where the catalogue is structurally likely to be incomplete.

E1 INGENIOUS compilation methodology
The INGENIOUS Great Basin compilation (Ayling et al. 2022) is a regional dataset compilation. Its methodology — field campaigns, remote-sensing passes, terrain/access constraints, what was and was not mapped — determines the completeness envelope. This methodology was not independently re-read this session; the compilation is cited via its DOI and the rules §3.3 attribution. The catalogue-gap reasoning below is therefore a structural prior based on known characteristics of regional Quaternary fault compilations, not a claim about INGENIOUS's specific field protocol.
https://doi.org/10.15121/1881483 — DOI as printed in rules §3.3. Project page: gbcge.org/current-projects/ingenious/ (link-only this session — flagged).
low — methodology not independently verified; marked unverified
E2 USGS QFFDB methodology and completeness characteristics
The USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database compiles Quaternary faults from published mapping, field investigations, and remote sensing. Known structural gaps: (a) sparse historical fieldwork in remote terrain — large parts of the Great Basin were mapped from air photos and limited field traverses, leaving subtle or partially-buried scarps unmapped; (b) younger cover masking scarps — basin-fill sediments and colluvium can bury fault expressions that 100 m-resolution data miss but 1 m DEMs can reveal; (c) remote terrain — the higher-elevation, less-accessed parts of the GeoDAWN footprint were less intensively surveyed. These are general characteristics of regional compilations of this type, not specific claims about individual QFFDB records.
https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/faults — read 2026-09-26 (QFFDB overview; specific methodology references not individually fetched).
This is the catalogue-gap prior: candidates in sparsely-surveyed, young-cover, or remote-terrain settings have a structural reason to be absent from the catalogue independent of whether they "look fault-like." A candidate with high evidence in a well-mapped, outcrop-exposed area is more likely to be a catalogue duplicate than a gap.
medium — general characteristics; specific QFFDB protocol not independently verified
E3 Sare et al. 2019 catalogue incompleteness statistic
Sare et al. 2019 (C1) reports that up to ~85% of swath pixels are unmapped in the Q-faults DB around real, field-dated scarps. This is the most directly relevant published number on catalogue completeness in a scarp-detection context, and it was read from the primary source.
From C1 above: doi:10.1029/2018JB016886 — §1, in-paper context.
high — primary, read in full
Catalogue-gap reasoning summary (our structural prior, labelled as such): the highest-value candidates are those in settings where the catalogue is structurally most likely to be incomplete — (1) remote / sparsely-field-worked terrain, (2) areas with young basin fill or colluvium that can mask scarps at 100 m but not at 1 m, (3) fault intersections and transfer zones (D3) where blind systems are found, (4) near Holocene-documented structures (the sourced records in CANDIDATE_GEOLOGY.md show Holocene faults in ≥3 of 6 queried windows — those windows have a geomorphic-visibility argument that others do not). Candidates that merely restate catalogue structure in well-mapped terrain are lower-value and are filtered by the `new_to_catalogue` >300 m criterion.

F. Prior art — published fault/lineament-extraction ML work

Published ML approaches applicable to this feature stack, and the reference solution's specific design choices and where they likely fall short.

F1 Reference solution design choices and limitations
The reference solution (A3) trains a U-Net MC-CV ensemble only on catalogue labels with TverskyLoss matching the competition metric. Its design choices: 5-fold MC-CV, 128 px patches, augmentation, combined prediction, threshold ~0.1. Where it falls short on the hidden target: (a) it cannot learn geometries absent from the catalogue by construction; (b) the 128 px patch size at 100 m resolution limits the spatial context available for long-trace continuity; (c) the Monte-Carlo split is random, not spatially blocked — the reference solution does not implement the spatial blocking the brief demands of our pipeline.
https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution — notebook read cell-by-cell 2026-09-26.
high — official reference, read in full
F2 Published ML fault/lineament extraction (survey-level)
Multiple published approaches exist for automated fault/lineament extraction from potential-field and DEM data: (a) deep learning on potential-field derivatives for fault detection (various authors, region-specific); (b) CNN-based lineament extraction from aeromagnetic grids; (c) U-Net and variant architectures for geological structure segmentation. The specific published studies most relevant to the GeoDAWN feature stack are the scarp-detection ML review (C5) and the iTilt reference (B4). A systematic survey of fault-detection ML on this exact feature stack was not completed this session — flagged as a gap.
C5: ijres.org scarp review (read). B4: Springer iTilt suite (read).
low — gap: systematic prior-art survey on this feature stack not completed
